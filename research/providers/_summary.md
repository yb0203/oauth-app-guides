# Summary: creating and approving a provider OAuth app, ten providers compared

Researched 2026-10-19. No providers were swapped; all ten requested providers were covered. Per-provider detail is in the files linked below. Evidence labels follow the same rule everywhere: TESTED (we saw it), DOCS (official provider docs), COMMUNITY (third party), UNCERTAIN. How the checks were done, and what could not be checked, is in `_verification.md`. Browser observations are in `_browser_observations.md`.

## Where the assumptions in the brief held up and where they did not

- **GitHub is instant: holds.** With the caveat that customer organizations approve third-party OAuth apps, on by default for new orgs.
- **Notion is instant: holds for OAuth.** Review applies only to the optional Marketplace listing. A caption in the docs ("populates after a public connection is submitted for review") is unexplained.
- **Google restricted Gmail scopes need a yearly third-party assessment: holds,** with a nuance. The trigger is accessing data "from or through a third-party server", and Google's AL1 and AL2 levels replace the old tier names. Google publishes no price.
- **Xero and Spotify cap unapproved apps at a small number: true but each is different from the assumption.** Xero's free cap is 5 connections, but a card lifts it to 50 with no review. Spotify's cap is 5 users (it was 25 until February 2026), and the owner needs Premium.

## Patterns that recur (candidate fields for the guide schema)

1. **Tier, and what the tier gates.** Four tiers fit, but the gate is rarely "the OAuth app". It is a feature (Slack history, Slack MCP), a count (Xero connections, HubSpot installs, Spotify users), a consent screen (Microsoft, Google unverified warning), or a listing. Fields: `tier`, `what_works_unreviewed`, `what_is_gated`.
2. **Unreviewed cap, with its exact number and its unit.** Users (Spotify 5, Google 100 new users for life, Meta role holders), connections or tenants (Xero 5 or 50), installs (HubSpot 25), workspaces (Slack, Marketplace needs 10), or none (GitHub, Notion). Fields: `unreviewed_cap_value`, `unreviewed_cap_unit`, `cap_scope` (per app, per developer, per customer org, per project lifetime).
3. **Customer-side gate, separate from the provider's review.** GitHub org OAuth restrictions, Microsoft tenant consent policies and admin consent, Slack workspace app approval, Notion Enterprise "approved only", Xero's five uncertified apps per customer org, HubSpot installer must be a Super Admin. Fields: `customer_admin_gate`, `who_must_approve`.
4. **Review trigger and review type.** Optional listing (Notion, GitHub, HubSpot for scale), mandatory for scale (Slack for commercial use, Shopify, Meta, Google restricted), certification (Xero Plus, Microsoft M365), or application only for organizations (Spotify). Fields: `review_trigger`, `review_kind`, `reviewer`.
5. **Submission artifacts.** Demo or screencast video (Google, Meta, Shopify, Slack recommended, HubSpot listing and certification), scope justification (Google, Slack, Xero, Shopify PCD), privacy policy and terms URLs (Google, Meta, Slack, HubSpot listing, Shopify), test credentials for the reviewer (Slack, Meta, Shopify, Xero), domain verification (Google Search Console, Microsoft publisher domain, HubSpot TXT record), business verification (Meta, Microsoft Partner Center), security assessment (Google CASA, Xero). Fields: `artifacts_required[]` with `kind`, `human_only`.
6. **Two waits, never the same.** Official versus community-reported wait, plus an "official figure exists" flag. Official numbers range from "none stated" (GitHub, Shopify) to explicit (Slack, HubSpot, Spotify, Notion). Community numbers were consistently longer in 2026 (Spotify, Shopify, Meta, Slack). Fields: `wait_official`, `wait_official_source`, `wait_community`, `wait_community_evidence_strength`.
7. **Cost.** Mostly none from the provider. Exceptions: Xero tier fees and a certification-linked platform fee, Shopify $19 registration (uncertain), Google CASA lab cost (no official figure, community $540 to $4,500), Spotify Premium for the owner. Fields: `provider_fee`, `third_party_cost`, `owner_subscription`.
8. **Token lifetime and refresh rules, with rotation and a hard stop.** Short access tokens everywhere (30 min to 12 hours). Refresh rules differ a lot: Xero 60 days single-use rotation, Spotify now 6 months absolute, Google 7 days in Testing and 100 per account, GitHub 6 months single-use, Slack opt-in 12 hours with single-use refresh, Shopify 1 hour and 90 days, Microsoft 90 days rolling, Meta 60 days refreshable, HubSpot 30 minutes and unstated, Notion unstated. Fields: `access_ttl`, `refresh_ttl`, `refresh_rotates`, `refresh_race_risk`, `reauth_required_after`.
9. **Redirect URI rules.** HTTPS required almost everywhere, with loopback or localhost exceptions that differ (Spotify allows 127.0.0.1 not localhost; Xero allows localhost not 127.0.0.1; Microsoft rejects `[::1]`; Slack docs silent). Wildcards allowed only in narrow cases (GitHub per-URL opt-in, Microsoft manifest-only). Fields: `https_required`, `loopback_rule`, `wildcards`, `max_uris`.
10. **Per-tenant addressing.** Shopify (per-shop authorize URL), HubSpot (EU host uncertain), Microsoft (`/common` vs tenant, never `/common` for admin consent), Slack (Enterprise Grid org install), Notion (page-level sharing at consent). Fields: `tenant_addressing`, `user_must_supply`.
11. **Scope model and recent breaking changes.** Granular vs coarse (Xero granular scopes from 2 Mar 2026, Slack granular, Google sensitive vs restricted classes, GitHub OAuth scopes vs GitHub App permissions, Notion capabilities with no scope strings). Fields: `scope_model`, `scope_classes`, `recent_breaking_change` with a date. Guides will go stale fast: Spotify changed rules in Nov 2024, Apr 2025, Feb 2026, Jun 2026 and Jul 2026.
12. **Eligibility and policy restrictions that block a connector platform specifically.** HubSpot token-brokering exclusion, Xero's ban on aggregating and supplying user data and on AI training, Google's AI/ML training ban and agent-confirmation language, Slack's commercial-distribution rule and MCP restriction, Spotify's AI/ML training ban. This is the most Composio-specific field. Fields: `platform_restrictions[]`, `risk_for_shared_app`, `byo_app_alternative_notes`.
13. **Agent versus human split.** The same steps are human-only everywhere: account sign-up, 2FA, accepting terms, payment, identity or business verification, recording a demo on a real account, supplying reviewer credentials, clicking submit, waiting. Agent-doable everywhere: reading docs, drafting privacy policy, terms and scope justifications, preparing field values, writing code for token refresh and webhooks. Fields: `agent_steps[]`, `human_steps[]` with a `reason` drawn from a small set (`credential`, `legal_acceptance`, `payment`, `identity_document`, `real_account_demo`, `irreversible_submit`, `elapsed_time`).
14. **Effort and calendar time as two numbers with a basis.** Active hours versus elapsed time. Every effort figure in these files is our estimate, not sourced, and says so. Fields: `effort_hours_range`, `calendar_range`, `estimate_basis` (`official`, `community`, `our_estimate`).
15. **Evidence label per field, plus the source date.** Needed on every field. Several provider docs are summarized or JavaScript-rendered, so a `quote_verified_live` flag is also useful.
16. **Things you can only learn by logging in.** Required form fields, logo limits, consent-screen warnings, 2FA requirements, and where a fee is charged were UNCERTAIN for nearly every provider. A live-agent guide should mark these as "check on first sign-in" rather than guess.

## Most important caveats for the next phase

- The three newest policy shifts (HubSpot's June 2026 legacy-app sunset, Spotify's February 2026 dev-mode cut, Xero's March 2026 tier and scope changes) all happened within the last eight months. A library of guides needs a visible "last verified" date, and the live agent should re-fetch the provider's official pages instead of trusting a stored guide.
- Where a platform like Composio supplies a shared OAuth app, several providers have rules that could apply to it (HubSpot, Xero, Slack, Google). Nothing here shows a provider enforcing those rules against a connector platform; it is only that the written terms are broad. Treat as UNCERTAIN and ask each provider before relying on it.
